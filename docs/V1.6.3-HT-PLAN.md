# KM v1.6.3 plan: completeness on the hypertableau fragment

Status: draft, written 2026-11-12 during v1.6.2. Roadmap row: "hypertableau
completeness (role hierarchies, inverses, chains; an executable search
producer)".

## 1. Where v1.6.2 leaves it

The OWL capstone of v1.6.2 (`owlReference_sound`, `owlReference_complete`)
covers OWL EL with role hierarchy, chains and transitivity, and OWL ALC
without any role axiom. Inverses, symmetry, role hierarchies together with
ALC constructors, transitivity together with universal restrictions, and
role chains together with ALC constructors are outside both reference
procedures. On the checkers-on suite these are the inputs the hypertableau
routes serve.

The survey of 2026-11-09 (`docs/history/SURVEY-HT-CB-COMPLETENESS-2026-11-09.md`)
found that the HT totality theorems are conditional on a producer family
that nothing constructs, and that the crux of constructing one is a
blocking-correctness obligation (`frontierAddress`) for which no proof
exists. Making the production search executable and proving that
obligation is the expensive path.

## 1a. How large the targets are (measured 2026-11-13)

From the v1.6.2-rc1 `KM_FRONTEND_CHECK` run
(`results/agents/2026-11-12/v162-rc1-frontend-check/results.tsv`):

- The 84 in-`F` inputs that are neither EL nor ALC (the v1.6.3 targets)
  declare between 4 and 264,019 classes: 17 declare at most 14, the other
  67 between 37 and 264,019 (median of all 84: about 9,800). The
  translation adds one definer per complex subexpression on top.
- The 25 ALC inputs that the v1.6.2 executable declines on its budget have
  between 18 and 829,082 concept names after translation; only 3 have
  fewer than 30.

Type elimination enumerates subsets of the concept names, so at any
budget it reaches the 17 small inputs and none of the others. Section 2
keeps the theorem; section 2a replaces the procedure.

## 2a. Procedure: executable CB saturation (chosen by the maintainer, 2026-11-13)

Instead of type elimination, decide the SRI normal form
(`lean/ContextCalculus/SRI/NormalForm.lean`) with a consequence-based
saturation in the style of Simančík, Kazakov and Horrocks (2011) for
ALCH, with inverse roles as in Bate et al. (2016): one context per
conjunction of concept names reached from a requested name, clauses
`⊓ body ⊑ ⊔ head` inside contexts, and Succ/Pred rules along role terms
that respect the role hierarchy and inverses. The work per context is
bounded by the clauses that mention its names, not by 2^n, which is why
km's own CB route classifies the large inputs.

Correction (2026-11-13, on reading the Lean files): the existing CB
completeness theorem (`CBSourceEqualityFreeCompleteness`) is stated for
km's decoded wire documents (contexts with work-queue counters, Skolem
`FCL` clauses, a checked literal order), and it concludes that each
context's candidate valuation satisfies the source instances, not that a
model exists. Driving it from a Lean procedure would mean rebuilding km's
CB engine in Lean. v1.6.3 does not do that. It uses the self-contained
procedure of section 2b, whose contexts are keyed by their seed as in CB,
and proves it exact directly. Worst case stays exponential (the problem
is EXPTIME-hard); the executable keeps a time budget.

## 2b. The procedure and its proof (design, 2026-11-13)

Notation over the SRI normal form with role inclusions only (chains of
length 1; ALCHI). `R ⊑* S` is the reflexive-transitive closure of the
inclusions, closed under `flip` on both sides. For a set of names `t`:

- `fwd(t, R) = { c | allRight a S c ∈ O, a ∈ t, R ⊑* S }`
- `back(s, R) = { c | allRight a S c ∈ O, a ∈ s, R.flip ⊑* S }`
- `compat(R, t, s) := fwd(t, R) ⊆ s ∧ back(s, R) ⊆ t`; then
  `compat(R.flip, t, s) ↔ compat(R, s, t)`.
- `t` is closed when every `gci body head` with `body ⊆ t` has
  `head ∩ t ≠ ∅` (an empty head is a clash).
- A set `G` of closed types is self-realising when every `t ∈ G` and
  `exRight a R b` with `a ∈ t` has some `s ∈ G` with `b ∈ s` and
  `compat(R, t, s)`. `Good t` := `t` lies in a self-realising set.

**Step 1, exactness of good types** (extends `CompletenessContext`):
the canonical interpretation on good types with `r(t, s) :=
compat(pos r, t, s)` models `O`, and the type of every element of a model
is good. Hence `EntailsSub O a b ↔ ∀ t, Good t → a ∈ t → b ∈ t`. Role
inclusions hold in the canonical model because `R ⊑* S` makes
`compat(R)` imply `compat(S)`.

**Step 2, the candidate set `G*`** (computed, never 2^n in practice).
`branches(σ)`: start from the seed `σ`; take the gcis in a fixed order;
each one whose body is contained in the current set is processed once,
adding a subset `Y` of its head with `Y ∪ (head ∩ current) ≠ ∅` (one
branch per choice; Horn clauses do not branch). Every result is closed.
Records carry a seed, a type and their origins. `G*` is the least set
closed under:

- root: `branches({a})` for each requested `a`;
- child: for `t ∈ G*` and `exRight a R b` with `a ∈ t`, the branches of
  `{b} ∪ fwd(t, R)`, with origin `(t, R, b)`;
- re-parent: a record with origin `(g, R, b)` and seed `σ`, and a later
  version `t` of `g`: the branches of `σ ∪ fwd(t, R)`, origin `(t, R, b)`;
- augment: a record `t` with seed `σ`, and a record `s` whose origin is
  `(g, R, b)` with `g` an earlier version of `t`: the branches of
  `σ ∪ back(s, R)`, same origin as `t`.

"Later version" is the reflexive-transitive closure of re-parent and
augment. All records live in a finite universe, so the worklist stops.

**Step 3, elimination.** `alive` is the greatest subset of `G*` that is
self-realising (delete records with an unwitnessed demand until stable).
The answer: `a ⊑ b` iff every alive type containing `a` contains `b`; `a`
is unsatisfiable iff no alive type contains `a`; the ontology is
inconsistent iff a fresh `⊤` probe is unsatisfiable.

**Soundness.** `alive` is self-realising, so its members are good (Step 1).

**Completeness (the core lemma).** Let `G` be self-realising with a
witness function, `t0 ∈ G`, `a ∈ t0`, `b ∉ t0`. Unravel `G` from `t0`
into the tree of demand paths; `tp(x)` is the type at path `x`. Define,
by recursion on `k`,
`m_{k+1}(x) = C_{tp(x)}({b_x} ∪ fwd(m_k(parent x), R_x) ∪ ⋃ back(m_k(y), R_y))`
over the children `y`, where `C_T(σ)` is the least superset of `σ` that
adds `head ∩ T` whenever a gci body is contained in it. `C_T` is monotone
and stays inside `T` when `σ ⊆ T`, so `m_k(x)` increases inside `tp(x)`
and stops at some `M(x)`. Then:

1. `{ M(x) }` is self-realising (every forward and back requirement
   appears in the seed one stage later);
2. `C_T(σ)` is one of `branches(σ)` (choose `Y = head ∩ T`);
3. by induction on `k`, `m_k(x)` is a record of `G*` with origin
   `(m_{k-1}(parent x), R_x, b_x)`: re-parent moves the stage-`k` record
   to the stage-`k` parent, and augment adds the children's stage-`k`
   back sets, whose origins are earlier versions;
4. hence `{ M(x) } ⊆ alive`, and `M(root)` contains `a` and not `b`.

**Why it is fast on real ontologies.** For Horn inputs there is one
branch per seed and the records are the contexts of an EL-style
saturation, shared across parents with the same seed; `fwd` and `back`
add the universal and inverse propagation that CB does with its Succ and
Pred rules. Disjunctions branch only where their bodies fire.

**SHI and SRI.** Transitivity and regular chains enter as extra
`allRight` clauses and fresh names (section 2), so Steps 1 to 3 do not
change.

## 2. The theorem, in the form of v1.6.0 to v1.6.2

As before, completeness comes from an exact reference procedure that runs
after the production routes decline, not from the production search:

```lean
theorem owlReference_complete (h : O.isEL ∨ O.isALC ∨ O.isSHI ∨ O.isSRI) :
    (owlReference O requested).isSome = true
```

with `owlReference_sound` unchanged in form. The new fragments:

- **ALCHI**: ALC with role inclusions, equivalent and inverse properties,
  `ObjectInverseOf` in class expressions, symmetry. Decided by type
  elimination over the closure of the TBox with a compatibility condition
  for role edges that respects the hierarchy and inverses: a type `t` may
  have an `r`-successor of type `t'` when, for every `s` with `r ⊑* s`,
  `∀s.D ∈ t` gives `D ∈ t'` and `∀s⁻.D ∈ t'` gives `D ∈ t`. ALCHI has the
  finite model property, so elimination over finite types is exact.
- **SHI**: ALCHI with transitive roles, reduced to ALCHI by the standard
  encoding: for every `∀s.C` in the closure and every transitive `t` with
  `t ⊑* s`, add `∀s.C ⊑ ∀t.∀t.C` (in clause form: the definer of `∀s.C`
  implies the definer of `∀t.∀t.C`). The reduction preserves concept
  satisfiability and subsumption between named classes (Tobies 2001,
  section 5.2); the Lean proof is the model-unravelling argument: close
  each transitive role of a model of the encoding transitively and show
  that every `∀` constraint survives.
- **SRI** (regular chains): role chains under the regularity condition of
  OWL 2, reduced to ALCHI by the automaton encoding (Horrocks, Kutz,
  Sattler 2006; Kazakov 2008): one definer per state of the role's
  automaton, propagated along edges. This is the same argument as the
  role-encoding equivalence of `docs/V1.6.2-FRONTEND-PLAN.md` section 5
  (phase B), and the two should share the closure-induction lemma.
