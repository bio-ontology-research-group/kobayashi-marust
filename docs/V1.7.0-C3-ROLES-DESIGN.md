# Stage C3: SROIQ role features (design)

Design note for stage C3 of `docs/V1.7.0-PLAN.md`, 2026-11-15. It extends
the proved SHOIQ(D) route of stage C2a
(`lean/ContextCalculus/SRIQN/`, `OWLFrontend/SHOIQDC2aReference.lean`) to
the role features of SROIQ: general regular role inclusion chains,
`ObjectHasSelf`, disjoint, reflexive, irreflexive and asymmetric object
properties, and the universal role. The plan is in four parts, in proof
order:

1. regularity and the automaton of a role (section 2, 3);
2. chain elimination at the normal-form level, with nominals and data
   (section 4), after which the C2a route applies unchanged;
3. the features that change the realisation: Self, irreflexive,
   asymmetric, disjoint (section 5);
4. the OWL route (section 6) and the recount (section 7).

Sources: W3C OWL 2 Structural Specification (second edition), sections
11.1 and 11.2; Horrocks, Kutz, Sattler, "The Even More Irresistible SROIQ"
(KR 2006), section 3 (regular RIAs and the automata `B_R`); Kazakov,
"RIQ and SROIQ are harder than SHOIQ" (KR 2008), for the size of the
automata and the role of regularity.

## 1. Notation

Role terms are `RoleT (Fin m)` (`pos r`, `inv r`, `flip`). An `SRIQ`
ontology carries the role axioms as `chain body S` clauses:

* inclusions, equivalences, inverses and symmetry: chains of length one;
* transitivity: `chain [T, T] T`;
* general chains: `chain [R₁, …, Rₙ] S`, `n ≥ 2`;
* **new:** reflexivity: `chain [] S` (the empty path is equality, so
  `chain [] S` is `∀x. S(x, x)`, the OWL semantics of
  `ReflexiveObjectProperty(S)`).

`R ⊑₁* S` is `SRIQ.RoleLe` (length-one chains, both directions under
`flip`), the relation `→*` of the structural specification.

**Rules.** `rules O` is the list of `(S, body)` for every chain clause with
`body.length ≠ 1`, together with the flipped rule
`(S.flip, (body.map flip).reverse)`. The flip is the inverse closure of
the specification (`OPE1 → OPE2` implies `INV(OPE1) → INV(OPE2)`, and a
chain `w ⊑ S` gives `inv(w) ⊑ S⁻` in every model).

**The language of a role.** `Gen O w G` (`w ⊑* G`) is the least relation
with

* `Gen O [R] G` if `R ⊑₁* G`;
* `Gen O (w₁ ++ … ++ wₖ) G` if `(T, [B₁, …, Bₖ]) ∈ rules O`, `T ⊑₁* G`
  and `Gen O wᵢ Bᵢ` for each `i` (for `k = 0`: `Gen O [] G`).

Facts (all by induction on `Gen`, no regularity needed):

* **G1, soundness.** In every model `I` of `O`, `Gen O w G` and
  `I.path w x y` give `I.rt G x y`.
* **G2, flip.** `Gen O w G ↔ Gen O (w.map flip).reverse G.flip`.
* **G3, closure.** `Gen O wᵢ Bᵢ` for the body of a chain clause with
  super-role `T` gives `Gen O (w₁ ++ … ++ wₖ) T`; `Gen O w R` and
  `R ⊑₁* G` give `Gen O w G`.
* **G4, simple roles.** If no rule head `T` has `T ⊑₁* G`, then
  `Gen O w G ↔ ∃ R, w = [R] ∧ R ⊑₁* G`.

## 2. Regularity (structural specification 11.2)

The specification asks for a strict partial order `<` on role terms with

* (R1) `R < S ↔ R.flip < S`;
* (R2) `R < S → ¬ S ⊑₁* R`;
* (R3) every chain `[B₁, …, Bₙ] ⊑ S`, `n ≥ 2`, satisfies one of:
  `n = 2 ∧ B₁ = B₂ = S`; `Bᵢ < S` for all `i`; `B₁ = S ∧ Bᵢ < S` for
  `i ≥ 2`; `Bₙ = S ∧ Bᵢ < S` for `i ≤ n − 1`.
  (The case `S = owl:topObjectProperty` is handled in section 6.)

`Regular O` is the existence of such an order.

**Decidable check.** For each chain the applicable option is determined
by its shape: `[S, S]` needs nothing; `B₁ = S` forces the fourth option
(the third and fifth would need `S < S`); otherwise `Bₙ = S` forces the
fifth; otherwise the third. Let `need O` be the pairs `(Bᵢ, S)` the forced
option requires, closed on the left under `flip`, and `ltC O` its
transitive closure. Then

```
regularB O = true  ↔  Regular O
regularB O := (∀ R, ¬ ltC O R R) ∧ (∀ R S, ltC O R S → ¬ S ⊑₁* R)
```

Proof idea: `ltC O` is transitive, inverse-closed on the left (R1, both
directions since `flip` is an involution) and contains the forced pairs
(R3); the check gives irreflexivity and R2. Conversely every valid order
contains the forced pairs (shape argument above) and is transitive and
inverse-closed, so it contains `ltC O`; irreflexivity and R2 pass to the
smaller relation. Reflexivity clauses (`chain []`) are not constrained.

**A gap in the specification's condition.** R2 only forbids `S ⊑₁* R`
for `R < S`; it does not ask that sub-roles of a smaller role be smaller.
The RBox `P ⊑ A`, `Q ⊑ B`, `C B C ⊑ P`, `A D ⊑ Q` is regular in the sense
above (order `B, C < P` and `A, D < Q`), yet `L(A) ⊇ C L(A) D C`, so
`{(C)ⁿ A (D C)ⁿ}` lies in it and `L(A)` is not regular. HKS avoid this
because their order covers role inclusions of length one too. KM's route
therefore needs a second, semantic gate: the automaton below is exact for
every RBox, and the route declines when its reachable state set is not
closed after the search bound (section 3.3). On a strongly regular RBox
(the order closed under `⊑₁*` on both sides) the state set is finite
(section 3.4).

## 3. The automaton of a role

### 3.1 Items and moves

The automaton is a pushdown machine whose stacks stay bounded on strongly
regular RBoxes. An item is `(S, b)`, a role term and a flag: `(S, false)`
is the initial state `i_S` of HKS's `B_S` ("an `S`-word is still to be
read"), `(S, true)` the final state `f_S` ("an `S`-word has been read; it
may be extended"). A state is a stack of items, top first. The moves of
a stack `(S, b) :: rest`:

| move | condition | new stack | letter |
|---|---|---|---|
| letter | `b = false`, `R ⊑₁* S` | `(S, true) :: rest` | `R` |
| call | `b = false`, `R ⊑₁* S`, `¬ S ⊑₁* R`, `R` a rule head | `(R, false) :: (S, true) :: rest` | – |
| reflexive | `b = false`, rule `(T, [])`, `T ⊑₁* S` | `(S, true) :: rest` | – |
| ordinary | `b = false`, rule `(T, B)`, `T ≈ S`, `B` not of the shapes below | `B.map (·, false) ++ (S, true) :: rest` | – |
| right | `b = false`, rule `(T, w ++ [T])`, `T ≈ S`, `w ≠ []` | `w.map (·, false) ++ (S, false) :: rest` | – |
| left | `b = true`, rule `(T, T :: w)`, `T ≈ S`, `w ≠ [T]`, `w ≠ []` | `w.map (·, false) ++ (S, true) :: rest` | – |
| trans | `b = true`, rule `(T, [T, T])`, `T ≈ S` | `(S, false) :: rest` | – |
| pop | `b = true` | `rest` | – |

`T ≈ S` is `T ⊑₁* S ∧ S ⊑₁* T`. The machine for `S` starts in
`[(S, false)]` and accepts in `[]`. These are the transitions of HKS's
`B_S` (`i_S →S f_S`, the paths `i_S →w i_S`, `f_S →w f_S`, `i_S →w f_S`,
`f_S →ε i_S`) with every transition on a smaller role replaced by a
call into that role's automaton (HKS's copies), realised as a push. Role
equivalence classes share one automaton, which is how the specification's
equivalent properties (allowed, unlike in HKS's order) are handled.

### 3.2 Language theorem

`Acc O st w`: the machine reads `w` from stack `st` to `[]`.

```
theorem acc_iff_gen : Acc O [(S, false)] w ↔ Gen O w S
```

for every ontology `O` (no regularity).

* **Soundness** by a concatenation invariant: let `M(S, false) = Gen(S)`,
  `M(S, true) = {v | ∀ u ∈ Gen(S), u ++ v ∈ Gen(S)}` and
  `M(st)` the concatenation. Every move shrinks the meaning (for a letter
  `R`: `R · M(st') ⊆ M(st)`), and `[] ∈ M([])`. Each case is G3.
* **Completeness** by induction on `Gen w G`, with the claim
  `∀ S rest, G ⊑₁* S → Run ((S, false) :: rest) w ((S, true) :: rest)`.
  Letter: one move. Rule `(T, B)` with `T ⊑₁* G ⊑₁* S`: if `¬ S ⊑₁* T`,
  call `T` and use the case `T ≈ T`; otherwise `T ≈ S` and the shape of
  `B` selects the move: ordinary (run each `Bᵢ` from its item and pop
  it), right (run `w`, then the last child by induction at `S`), left
  (the first child by induction at `S`, then run `w`), trans (first
  child, `trans`, second child), reflexive (one move).

### 3.3 The finite automaton

For the encoding the states must be finite. `reach O S fuel` is the
breadth-first closure of `[[(S, false)]]` under the moves (stacks up to
the search bound); `closedB` checks that every successor of a listed
stack is listed. With `closedB`, every run from `[(S, false)]` stays in
the list (`run_mem`), which is all the encoding needs. The route declines
when `closedB` fails.

### 3.4 Finiteness (pen proof, not in Lean)

On a strongly regular RBox, every pushed item is for a role strictly
smaller (in `<` or strictly below in `⊑₁*`) than the item it is pushed
above, except the items of one rule body, which share their owner. So a
stack is a sequence of segments, each a suffix of one rule body followed
by its owner, with owners strictly decreasing; the depth is at most
(longest body) × (number of role classes), and the reachable set is
finite. Its size is exponential in the RBox, as HKS's `B_S` and as
Kazakov shows is unavoidable.

## 4. Chain elimination (generalising `encTransQ` and `encTransN`)

Fix an `SRIQ` ontology `O` and for every `allRight a S c ∈ O` the finite
automaton of `S` (section 3.3). The encoding `encChain O`:

* keeps every clause of `O` except chains of length other than one;
* adds a fresh name `X(a,S,c,st)` per listed stack `st`, with
  `gci [a] [X(a,S,c,[(S,false)])]`; `allRight X(st) R X(st')` per letter
  move; `gci [X(st)] [X(st')]` per silent move; `gci [X([])] [c]`.

The original `allRight a S c` stays (it is redundant, and keeping it keeps
`RoleLe` and the C2a quantities unchanged).

**Simple roles (strengthened).** `SimpleC O R`: no rule `(T, B)` (any
length other than one, so reflexivity included) has `T ⊑₁* R` or
`T ⊑₁* R.flip`. Required for the roles of `atLeast`/`atMost`. This is the
specification's simplicity plus: no reflexive sub-role. A reflexive
sub-role makes the number restriction count the element itself, which the
star conditions of C1 and C2 do not model; the route declines (deviation
from OWL 2 DL, which allows reflexive simple properties in cardinalities).

**Up** (`upC`): roles unchanged, old names unchanged,
`X(a,S,c,st) x := ∀ w y, Acc O st w → I.path w x y → I.c c y`. The
clauses hold by G1 and `acc_iff_gen` (→).

**Down** (`downC`): old names unchanged,
`r := fun x y => ∃ w, Gen O w (pos r) ∧ J.path w x y`. Then
`downC.rt R x y ↔ ∃ w, Gen O w R ∧ J.path w x y` (G2), every chain of
`O` holds (G3), reflexivity holds (`Gen [] S`), `allRight a S c` holds by
`acc_iff_gen` (←), `run_mem` and induction on the run, and for a simple
role `downC.rt R = J.rt R` (G4 and the length-one chains kept in the
encoding), so counting clauses transfer.

```
theorem encChain_correct  : SimpleCounts O → closedAll O →
    (EntailsSub O a b ↔ EntailsSub (encChain O) a' b')
theorem consistentN_encChainN  : … ConsistentN O ↔ ConsistentN (encChainN O)
theorem consistentDN_encChainDN : … ConsistentDN Θ O ↔ ConsistentDN Θ (encChainDN O)
```

Nominal clauses hold on both sides because both maps keep the old names;
data clauses mention only concept names and data properties. The result
is ALCHOIQ (only length-one chains remain), so it is SHIQ with no
transitive role, and `consistentPartsN_iff` (C2a with data) applies to it
directly; its internal transitivity removal adds no names.

## 5. Self, irreflexive, asymmetric and disjoint roles

All four are on simple roles (structural specification 11.2), and with
reflexivity removed into chains (section 4) they act on single edges:

| axiom | normal form |
|---|---|
| `ObjectHasSelf(R)` positive, result `X` | `selfR X R` (`X ⊑ ∃R.Self`) |
| `ObjectHasSelf(R)` negative, result `X` | `selfL R X` (`∃R.Self ⊑ X`) |
| `IrreflexiveObjectProperty(R)` | `selfL R ⊥` |
| `AsymmetricObjectProperty(R)` | `disj R R.flip` |
| `DisjointObjectProperties(R, S)` | `disj R S` |
| `ReflexiveObjectProperty(R)` | `chain [] R` (section 4) |

Asymmetry is disjointness of `R` and its inverse: `R(x,y) ∧ R(y,x)` is
`R(x,y) ∧ R⁻(x,y)`.

**Star change.** A record gets a *self label* `σ`: a set of role terms,
up-closed under `⊑₁*` and closed under `flip` (a loop `R(x,x)` is also
`R⁻(x,x)`). The star of a node gains one entry `(σ, t)` for its own type
when `σ ≠ ∅`. `StarOK` then reads:

* `selfR a R`: `a ∈ t → R ∈ σ`; `selfL R a`: `R ∈ σ → a ∈ t`;
* `EdgeOK t (σ, t)` for universal clauses (`∀R.c` with `R ∈ σ` puts `c`
  in `t`);
* counting: the self entry counts once for `R ∈ σ, b ∈ t`;
* `disj R S`: no label of an entry (self label included) contains both
  `R` and `S`.

In the forest model of C2a (`realisation_sound`), a tree node gets the
loop `σ`, core elements keep `lab c c` (which must then equal the core
self label). Lemma S1 becomes `I.rt R x y ↔ R ∈ label(x, y)` with
`label(x, x) = σ`; S2 and S3 gain the self entry, which is distinct from
every other neighbour. The disjointness conditions are conditions on
labels only, so S1 gives them in the model. In the completeness
direction the labels come from the model (`labelOf I x y`, the self label
is `labelOf I x x`), so every condition holds; witness labels
(`wl x y ⊆ labelOf I x y`) keep the disjointness conditions because they
are subsets. The C2a condition is unchanged: a self loop is not an edge to
another element, so it creates no shared anonymous element.

Correctness theorems (generalising the C2a ones; the old statements are
the case with no new clauses, `σ = ∅`):

```
theorem realisationS_sound    : RealisationS O K → ConsistentN O
theorem c2aS_complete         : IsC2a O → ConsistentN O → ∃ k, … RealisationS O (Fin k)
```

## 6. The OWL route

Fragment `Ontology.isSROIQDKB`: the SHOIQ(D) knowledge-base part plus
`SubObjectPropertyOf` with any nonempty chain of basic properties,
`ReflexiveObjectProperty`, and (after section 5) `ObjectHasSelf`,
`IrreflexiveObjectProperty`, `AsymmetricObjectProperty`,
`DisjointObjectProperties`, all over basic properties. SWRL rules and
keys stay out (later stages).

Translation without touching the SHIQ(D) translation: `roleOff O`
replaces each new role axiom by the inclusions `SubObjectPropertyOf(r r)`
for its properties (true in every interpretation, and they make the
translation index the properties), translates with `translateSHOIQD`, and
appends the role clauses built from the translation's role table
(`rnames`). This needs the model maps of the knowledge-base translation
to state that the role of index `k` is the property it stands for, in both
directions; the maps already have this inside (`extend_prop`,
`restrict_prop`), and the strengthening adds it to the statements.
`ObjectHasSelf(r)` is abstracted to a fresh class name `X_r` with the
clause pair of section 5 on the translated side.

Capstones: `owlSROIQDC2aConsistent_sound` (every answer is consistency at
`owl2MapP pat`) and `_isSome_iff` (answers exactly on `isSROIQDKB` with
the checks: regularity, closed automata, strengthened simple roles, C2a,
supported data), and the six rows (type, same, subsumption, different,
role, negative role) by the consistency reductions of the C2a route.
CLI: `km-owl-reference --procedure sroiq-c2a`.

**Universal role.** OWL 2 DL allows `owl:topObjectProperty` in class
expressions and as the super-property of inclusions and chains. As a
super-property it is vacuous (every pair is related) and the axiom is
dropped. In a class expression, `∃U.C` and `∀U.C` (and the cardinalities)
reduce to a fresh individual `o` and a fresh property `u`:
`SubClassOf(owl:Thing ObjectHasValue(u o))`, and `U` replaced by the path
`u ∘ u⁻` (`∃U.C ↦ ∃u.∃u⁻.C`, `≥n U.C ↦ ∃u.≥n u⁻.C`). In every model
`u⁻` relates `o` to every element, so the path is the universal relation;
conversely interpret `u` as `D × {o}`. `u` occurs in no at-most clause, so
C2a is unaffected.

5. Section 6 (route, CLI, tests) and section 7.

## 9. Open points

* **U1** (section 3.4) finiteness of the automaton on strongly regular
  RBoxes is a pen proof; the route checks closure instead.
* **U2** (section 2) the specification's regularity admits RBoxes with
  non-regular languages; these fail the closure check and are declined.

## Deviations (Lean, stage C3, 2026-11-15)

Proved in `lean/ContextCalculus/SROIQ/` and routed in
`lean/ContextCalculus/OWLFrontend/SROIQD{Nom,C2aReference}.lean`. Where the
Lean statements differ from sections 1 to 8:

* **D1, rules (section 1).** `Gen` takes the hierarchy `⊑₁*` as letters and
  every chain of length other than one (reflexivity included) as a rule,
  with its flip. The children of a rule are a list of words zipped with the
  body (no nested inductive).
* **D2, the language theorem (section 3.2)** holds for every ontology
  (`acc_iff_gen`), so regularity is not a hypothesis of any correctness
  theorem. Regularity (`regularB_iff`) is a fragment gate of the route
  only. Finiteness of the automaton (U1) is not proved; the route checks
  closure of the listed stacks (`closedB`, at most 64 rounds and 4096
  stacks per role).
* **D3, simple roles (section 4).** `SimpleC O R` asks only that no rule
  head lies below `R` (rules are closed under flip, so `R.flip` needs no
  separate condition). Universal clauses over a role with no rule below
  it keep their clause and get no automaton names (`allsQ` filters them);
  this is only an economy.
* **D4, the OWL route (section 6)** covers general chains, reflexivity and
  `SubObjectPropertyOf(… owl:topObjectProperty)` (vacuous). The role table
  of the translation is read through `translateSRIQDKB_mapsR`, a
  strengthening of `translateSRIQDKB_maps` (which stays, as a corollary),
  and through new conjuncts of `SRIQKB.core_up` and `SRIQKB.core_down`.
  The role-table lookups of `roleCls` are a check of the route, not proved
  to succeed.
* **D5, section 5 not done.** `ObjectHasSelf`, irreflexive, asymmetric and
  disjoint properties are outside `isSROIQDKB`; the route declines them
  ("not recognised"). The open piece is the realisation with self labels
  and edge conditions (`realisationS_sound`, `c2aS_complete`), the
  extension of `SRIQN/Core.lean`, `Forest.lean`, `C2a.lean` and the search
  in `Procedure.lean`.
* **D6, the universal role in class expressions (section 6)** is not
  routed; `owl:topObjectProperty` inside a class expression is declined
  (`ObjProp.isBasic`).
* **D7, tests.** The decisions on the test inputs need hints: the
  unhinted exhaustive C2a search exceeds 120 s on each of them (as the
  SHOIQ route does on a nine-name input). The automaton and the
  regularity check are tested by `#guard` in `SROIQ/Tests.lean`;
  `testdata/sroiq/expected.tsv` records the fragment checks and declines.
* **D8, evaluation.** The executable code of the encoding and the checks
  uses equal definitions registered with `@[csimp]` (`SROIQ/ChainFast.lean`:
  a super-role table computed once, the pair list computed once). Each
  replacement is a proved equality; no theorem changes.
