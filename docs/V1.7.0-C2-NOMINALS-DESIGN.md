# Stage C2: SHOIQ knowledge bases with nominals (design)

Design note for stage C2 of `docs/V1.7.0-PLAN.md`, 2026-11-14. It extends
the proved stages C1 (`lean/ContextCalculus/SRIQ/Stars.lean`,
`entailsSub_iff_star`), C1b (`SRIQ/ABox.lean`, `SRIQ/ABoxProcedure.lean`,
`consistentKB_iff_realisation`, `consistentKB_iff`) and C4 (`SRIQD/`) to
nominals. It contains no Lean proofs. The prototype is in
`tools/c2-proto/`, its validation in section 9.

## 0. Summary

**Theorem (C2).** Let `O` be an ALCHOIQ ontology in normal form (the SRIQ
clauses with role chains of length one, plus nominal clauses `nom a`).
`O` is consistent iff it has a *realisation*: a finite nonempty *core*
(types, a symmetric label matrix, every nominal name in exactly one core
type) in
which every core element has a star whose remaining entries are tree
children with records that are *good relative to the core*. Tree records
may have edges to core elements ("core entries"), but only edges that no
at-most clause at the core element counts. Moreover a realisation exists
with at most

```
B(O) = max(1, |Nom|) · (b^(℘+1) − 1)/(b − 1)        (b ≥ 2; ℘+1 if b ≤ 1)
b    = sum of the numbers of the at-most clauses of O
℘    = P + 2,  P = (number of tree types) · (number of labels) · (number of tree types)
```

core elements. `B(O)` is doubly exponential in `|O|`.

**Source of the bound.** The proof transfers the termination argument of
the hypertableau calculus (Motik, Shearer, Horrocks, JAIR 36, 2009,
Lemma 7: root individuals form, per named individual, a tree of depth at
most ℘ and branching at most `b`; ℘ comes from pairwise blocking) to a
construction steered by a model. That argument refines the NN-rule
termination of Horrocks and Sattler (JAR 39, 2007; IJCAI 2005). The
NExpTime results of Tobies (JAIR 12, 2000, for ALCQIO with unary numbers)
and Pratt-Hartmann (2005, C2 with binary numbers) suggest a singly
exponential core suffices, but their proofs go through C2 and integer
programming over star-type multiplicities (Pratt-Hartmann's "frames"),
not through a core; I did not port them (section 12, item U5).

**Confidence.** The soundness direction (realisation to model) is a direct
extension of the C1b forest construction; high confidence. The
completeness direction with the bound (section 3.4) is a pen proof that
follows Motik et al. step by step; I checked it twice and the prototype
validates its finite case, but it is the least certain part (section 12
lists the steps). The C2a sub-case (section 7) needs no bound and no
blocking; high confidence.

**Validation (section 9).** 38 hand cases (the Motik et al. examples
(5), (6), (8), the Rudolph–Glimm three-nominal cycle, NN-rule guessing
cases, inverse functional roles with nominals, unnamed elements forced
equal to a nominal, infinite models, promoted core elements) and 500
random normal-form knowledge bases (1,755 queries). The exhaustive
procedure decided 31 hand cases and 1,682 random queries and agrees with
HermiT 1.4.3 on all of them; the rest hit the Python time limit (all
random time-outs are inconsistent queries). On all 1,124 queries with a
finite model, both the model-to-certificate map and the steered
construction of section 3.4 produce certificates that check (28 needed
promotions). km agrees with HermiT wherever it answers. No disagreement
remains.

**C2a (section 7).** No shared anonymous element arises when no lower
clause on a non-nominal subject has a role `S` with `S ⊑* R⁻` for the role
`R` of some at-most clause. Then the core is the set of nominal elements
and no promotion, blocking or bound is needed. On the pool, 208 of the 276
analysed inputs with nominals are in C2a (75%), plus the 65 inputs that
use only `SameIndividual`/`DifferentIndividuals` (already C1b).

**Sources read.** Motik, Shearer, Horrocks, "Hypertableau Reasoning for
Description Logics", JAIR 36 (2009), sections 3.2.3–3.2.5 and the proofs
of Lemmas 6 and 7 (NI-rule, levels, termination); Rudolph and Glimm,
"Nominals, Inverses, Counting, and Conjunctive Queries", JAIR 39 (2010),
sections 1–2 (new nominals, infinitely many in some models);
Pratt-Hartmann, "Complexity of the Two-Variable Fragment with (Binary
Coded) Counting Quantifiers" (arXiv cs/0411031, 2004; JoLLI 2005),
sections 1–5 (frames of star-types, integer programming); Horrocks, Kutz,
Sattler, "The Even More Irresistible SROIQ" (KR 2006); Tobies, PhD thesis
(RWTH Aachen 2001, arXiv cs/0106031), chapter 5 (ALCQIO concept
satisfiability is NExpTime-complete with unary numbers, upper bound by
translation into C2 and Pacholski et al.; no structural model bound;
the infinity example `(≥1 ¬A), (∀ (∃R.⊤ ⊓ ≤1 R⁻ ⊓ ∀R.A))`). Horrocks and
Sattler's SHOIQ tableau (IJCAI 2005, JAR 2007) was not available to me in
full text (paywall, dead author link); I use its NN-rule as restated and
refined by Motik et al. (their "NI-rule", which reserves `n` root slots per
at-most restriction at a root, as the NN-rule's guessed nominals do).

## 1. Normal form with nominals

New namespace `ContextCalculus.SRIQN` (new files; `SRIQ` untouched).

```
inductive Clause (CName Role)
  | base (cl : SRIQ.Clause CName Role)      -- gci, exRight, allRight, atLeast, atMost, chain
  | nom (a : CName)                         -- a is interpreted as exactly one element
```

`satClause I (.nom a) := ∃ x, ∀ y, I.c a y ↔ y = x`; the other clauses as
in `SRIQ.satClause`. `Nom O = {a | nom a ∈ O}` (the nominal names).
`ConsistentN O := ∃ D (I : Interp D CName Role), models I O` (a nominal
makes `D` nonempty; with no nominal require `Nonempty D`).
`EntailsSubN O a b` as `SRIQ.EntailsSub` (nonempty domains). `IsALCHOIQ O`:
chains of length one (transitivity removed first, section 8).

**ABox as nominal clauses.** For an `SRIQ.ABox` over individuals `Ind`
(finite), add one fresh name `N_i` per individual with `nom N_i`, and

| assertion | clauses |
|---|---|
| `cls i A` | `gci [N_i] [A]` |
| `role i R j` | `exRight N_i R N_j` |
| `nrole i R j` | `allRight N_i R nN_j`, `gci [N_j, nN_j] []`, `gci [] [N_j, nN_j]` (fresh `nN_j`) |
| `same i j` | `gci [N_i] [N_j]`, `gci [N_j] [N_i]` |
| `diff i j` | `gci [N_i, N_j] []` |

`encNom_correct`: `SRIQ.ConsistentKB O A ↔ ConsistentN (encNom O A)`, and
`EntailsSubKB` likewise on the old names (interpret `N_i` as `{ind i}`
and `nN_j` as its complement; conversely `ind i` := the element of
`N_i`). This also embeds C1b.

## 2. Definitions

Fix `O` with `IsALCHOIQ O`. Types (`Finset CName`), labels
(`Finset (RoleT Role)`, up-closed under `RoleLe O`), `Nbr`, `EdgeOK`,
`cnt`, `StarOK`, `flipL` are those of `SRIQ/Stars.lean`. A *tree type* is
a closed type with no nominal name.

**2.1 Counted.** For a centre type `u`, a label `L` from the centre to a
neighbour and the neighbour's type `t`:

```
Counted O u L t := ∃ a k R b, atMost a k R b ∈ O ∧ a ∈ u ∧ R ∈ L ∧ b ∈ t
```

An entry that is not counted at its centre does not change any at-most
count there, however often it occurs.

**2.2 Core.** A core over a finite type `K` is `ty : K → Finset CName`
(closed) and `lab : K → K → Finset (RoleT Role)` with `lab c d` up-closed
(or empty) and `lab d c = flipL (lab c d)`, such that every nominal name
lies in `ty c` for exactly one `c` (`NomOK`). The core neighbours of `c`
are `nbrs ty lab c` (as in `SRIQ/ABox.lean`: one entry `(lab c d, ty d)`
per `d` with a nonempty label, `d = c` included).

**2.3 Records relative to a core.** A record is

```
RecN K := Finset CName × (Nbr ⊕ (K × Finset (RoleT Role)))
```

`(t, inl (L, s))`: a tree node of type `t` whose parent is a tree node of
type `s`, `L` the label from the node to the parent (exactly the `SRIQ`
non-root record). `(t, inr (c, L))`: an *anchor record*, a child of core
element `c`, `L` the label from the node to `c`. Its parent entry is
`(L, ty c)`.

A *core entry list* for a record `ρ = (t, p)` is a list `E` of pairs
`(d, L)` with distinct `d`, `d` not the anchor of `ρ`, `L` up-closed and
nonempty, `EdgeOK t (L, ty d)`, and `¬ Counted O (ty d) (flipL L) t`. Its
entries are `(L, ty d)`.

`SupportsN G ρ E C`: `t` has no nominal name, `E` is a core entry list for
`ρ`, and `StarOK O t (parent entry :: E.entries ++ C)`, and
`childRec t e ∈ G` for every `e ∈ C` (children are `SRIQ` neighbours
`(L, s)`; the child record is `(s, inl (flipL L, t))`).
`SelfSuppN G`, `GoodN ρ` as `SelfSupp`, `Good`.

Goodness relative to a core depends on `ty` only (not on `lab`), and on
`K` only through `ty` and the exclusion of the anchor.

**2.4 Realisation.** `RealisationN O K`: a core `(ty, lab)` over `K` with
`NomOK`, and for every `c : K` a list `C c` of neighbours with

```
StarOK O (ty c) (nbrs ty lab c ++ C c)   and   ∀ (L, s) ∈ C c, GoodN (s, inr (c, flipL L))
```

`K` is nonempty (it contains a nominal element, or the realisation picks
one element when `Nom O = ∅`).

**2.5 Minimal labels.** `NeedR O` = the roles of the `exRight`/`atLeast`
clauses and their flips. `shrink L := up (L ∩ NeedR O)`. Then
`shrink L ⊆ L`, `shrink (flipL L) = flipL (shrink L)`, and an entry
counts for a lower clause iff its shrunk entry does.

## 3. The characterisation

### 3.1 Statements

```
theorem realisation_sound   (h : IsALCHOIQ O) : RealisationN O K → ConsistentN O
theorem realisation_complete (h : IsALCHOIQ O) : ConsistentN O →
    ∃ k ≤ B O, Nonempty (RealisationN O (Fin k))
theorem consistentN_iff_realisation (h : IsALCHOIQ O) :
    ConsistentN O ↔ ∃ k ≤ B O, Nonempty (RealisationN O (Fin k))
```

`B O` as in section 0, computed from the clause list (`Fintype` counts of
tree types and labels over `Fin n`, `Fin m`).

### 3.2 Soundness: realisation to model (the forest)

Given a realisation, fix by choice a support `(E ρ, C ρ)` for every good
record `ρ` (inside one self-supporting set `G` containing all records
used; take `G` = all `GoodN` records, which is self-supporting).

Domain: `K ⊕ (K × ℕ × List ℕ)` restricted to valid nodes, as `FR` in
`SRIQ/ABox.lean`: `(c, i, path)` is the node reached from core element
`c` through its `i`-th child and then the child indices in `path`; its
record is computed as `recT` does. Interpretation:

* `I.c A (inl c) ↔ A ∈ ty c`; `I.c A node ↔ A ∈` the type of its record;
* role edges (`rawRT`): core–core by `lab`; core `c` – its `i`-th child by
  the child's label; tree parent–child by the child label; **new**: tree
  node `x` – core element `d` for every core entry `(d, L) ∈ E (rec x)`,
  with label `L` from `x` (and `flipL L` from `d`).

Lemmas (each with the role of its `SRIQ/ABox.lean` counterpart):

* **S1 (`forestN_rt`).** `I.rt R x y` iff `R` is in the label of the edge
  `x → y` given above (no edge: false). Labels are up-closed, so length-one
  chains hold (`roleLe_sound` direction).
* **S2 (neighbour lists of tree nodes).** The neighbours of a tree node
  `x` with record `ρ` are exactly: its parent (a tree node, or its anchor
  core element), one core element per entry of `E ρ`, and one child per
  entry of `C ρ`; they are pairwise distinct (children are distinct paths;
  core elements differ from tree nodes; the anchor is excluded from
  `E ρ`; core entries have distinct `d`). So the entries of the star of
  `x` in `I` are `parent :: E ρ ++ C ρ` up to permutation, and `StarOK`
  gives every clause at `x` (`starOK_perm`, as `nodeOK_tree`).
* **S3 (neighbour lists of core elements).** The neighbours of `c` are its
  core neighbours (`lab c d ≠ ∅`), its children `C c`, and *back
  neighbours*: tree nodes `x` with a core entry `(c, L)`. Every back
  neighbour is uncounted at `c` (definition of core entry lists), so for
  every at-most clause firing at `c` the count over all neighbours equals
  the count over `nbrs ++ C c`, and `StarOK` bounds it. Existential and
  at-least clauses are met by `nbrs ++ C c` alone. Universal clauses over a
  back edge hold by `EdgeOK t (L, ty c)` read in the reverse direction
  (the second conjunct of `EdgeOK`). Gcis: `ty c` is closed.
* **S4 (nominals).** A nominal name holds at exactly one core element
  (`NomOK`) and at no tree node (tree types have no nominal name), so
  `nom a` holds.
* **S5 (`forestN_models`).** From S1–S4 and `satClause_iff_satAt`
  (clauses are local), `models I O`.

A back neighbour can have infinitely many copies (a record can occur in
unboundedly many tree positions); S3 is the only place where that
matters, and it holds because the entry is uncounted. This is exactly
why counted inverse edges into the core must come from core elements or
anchor children.

### 3.3 Minimal labels (used by the procedures)

**Lemma L (`realisation_shrink`).** If `RealisationN O K` holds with labels
`lab` and supports `(E, C)`, it holds with every label replaced by its
`shrink` (core labels, core entries, child and parent labels). Proof:
lower-clause counts are unchanged (a counting role is in `NeedR`, and
`shrink` keeps it), at-most counts can only drop, `EdgeOK` and the
uncounted condition are monotone under smaller labels, and up-closure and
`flipL` commute with `shrink`. Entries whose label becomes empty count
for no lower clause at either end and are removed (`starOK_filter`).
Consequence: the searches range over the labels `up S` with
`∅ ≠ S ⊆ NeedR O` only.

### 3.4 Completeness with the bound: the steered construction

Fix a model `I` of `O` over `D`. For each `x` fix a duplicate-free witness
list `wit x` (as `Stars.wit`: for every `exRight`/`atLeast` clause firing
at `x`, `k` distinct witnesses), and record for each witness `y` the set
`need x y` of roles `R` of the clauses that `y` witnesses at `x`.

**Witness labels.** `wl x y := up (need x y ∪ flipL (need y x))`. Then
`wl x y ⊆ labelOf I x y`, `wl y x = flipL (wl x y)`, and every
`exRight`/`atLeast` clause at `x` is witnessed through edges whose witness
label contains its role. All labels below are witness labels; with them,
a structure in which every element's neighbours are distinct model
neighbours of its image and contain its witnesses satisfies `StarOK` at
every node (as `starOK_of_model`, using that fewer roles only weaken
at-most and universal conditions).

**Structures.** A structure `S` consists of

* a finite set `R ⊆ D` of *roots*, containing `Nom^I` (the elements of
  the nominal names; if `Nom O = ∅`, one fixed element `x0`), with an
  *introduction parent* `ip : R \ Nom^I → R` such that `y` is counted at
  `ip y` in `I` (`Counted O (type (ip y)) (wl (ip y) y) (type y)`), and
  `idep y` = depth of `y` in the introduction forest;
* a finite forest of *tree nodes* below the roots. Each node `ν` has an
  image `π ν ∈ D \ Nom^I`, a parent (a root or a node), a depth
  (`1` below a root) and *slots*: one per element of
  `wit (π ν) \ {π (parent ν)}` (for roots: `wit r`). A slot is `empty`,
  `child ν'` (a node with `π ν' = w`) or `core w` (an edge to the root
  `w ∈ R`).
* Edges carry witness labels of the images, except that a `core d` slot of
  a node `ν` carries `up (need (π ν) d)`, the node's own needs only.

*Pattern* of a node `ν` of depth ≥ 2: `(type (π ν), wl (π ν) (π parent),
type (π parent))`; this is the `SRIQ` record of the node. `ν` is
*blocked* if a proper ancestor of depth ≥ 2 has the same pattern, or an
ancestor is blocked. Blocking depends only on the ancestors, which never
change while `ν` exists.

**Rules** (applied in any order until none applies):

1. *Expand.* A root `r` with an empty slot `w`: `core w` if `w ∈ R`,
   else a new child `ν'` with `π ν' = w`. An unblocked node `ν` with an
   empty slot `w`: `core w` if `w ∈ Nom^I`, else a new child.
2. *NI (promotion).* A node `ν` with a slot `core d` that is counted at
   `d`: `Counted O (type d) (flipL (up (need (π ν) d))) (type (π ν))`. Let `y := π ν`. If `y ∉ R`, add `y` to `R` with `ip y := d`;
   if `y ∈ R` and `idep y > idep d + 1`, set `ip y := d`. Delete `ν` with
   its subtree; the parent's slot `y` becomes `core y`. Delete every child
   of a root whose image is `y`, with its subtree, and set that root slot
   to `core y`.

`y` is counted at `d` (that is the rule's condition), so `ip` stays legal.
Images of nodes are never nominal elements (nominal witnesses become core
slots), so `y ∉ Nom^I`.

**Lemma C1 (depth).** Every node has depth at most ℘ = P + 2, and a node
of depth ℘ is blocked. Proof: the nodes of depths 2, …, ℘ on a path are
P + 1 nodes with at most P patterns, so two share a pattern and the deeper
one is blocked. Blocked nodes get no children, so no node of depth ℘ + 1
exists, and a node of depth ℘ is itself the blocked one (otherwise an
ancestor would be blocked and the node would not exist). Witness labels
are minimal labels (section 2.5), so P counts minimal labels only.

**Lemma C2 (invariant).** At all times, for every node `ν` and slot
`core d` of `ν` that is counted at `d`: `idep d + depth ν ≤ ℘`.
Proof by induction over rule applications. Expand creates core slots
only to nominal elements (`idep = 0`) on nodes of depth ≤ ℘ (C1).
NI creates one new core slot, at the parent `p` of `ν` (if `p` is a
node), pointing to `y`; afterwards `idep y ≤ idep d + 1`, and
`depth p = depth ν − 1`, so `idep y + depth p ≤ idep d + depth ν ≤ ℘` by
the invariant for `ν`'s slot `core d`. Re-parenting only lowers `idep`;
deletions remove slots. (This is the measure `lev(s) + dep(t) ≤ ℘` of
Motik et al., Lemma 7, Claim 2, with the static introduction depth in
place of their dynamic level; see U2 in section 12 for why.)

**Lemma C3 (roots).** Every root has `idep ≤ ℘`, and every root has at most
`b` introduction children. So `|R| ≤ B(O)`. Proof: at its introduction,
`idep y = idep d + 1` with `idep d + depth ν ≤ ℘` and `depth ν ≥ 1`; `idep`
never grows. The introduction children of `d` are distinct elements of
`D`, each counted at `d` in `I`, so at most the sum of the numbers of the
at-most clauses that fire at `d`.

**Lemma C4 (termination).** Every run is finite. Proof: a *position* is a
root with a sequence of slot indices of length ≤ ℘ (finitely many: `R` is
bounded by C3, `|wit x| ≤ bnd O`). A position is created at most once:
a node is deleted only by NI on itself, an ancestor, or a root slot,
and then the slot above the deleted subtree is `core`, which never
returns to `empty` (root slots: `R` only grows; node slots: only NI
writes `core`). Each NI deletes at least one node, so the number of NI
steps is at most the number of creations.

**Lemma C5 (final structure).** When no rule applies:

* every root slot and every slot of an unblocked node is `child` or
  `core`;
* no node has a counted `core` slot (NI does not apply);
* no root has a child whose image is a root (NI's last clause, and
  Expand never creates one).

**Lemma C6 (reading the realisation).** Let `K := R`, `ty r := type r`,
`lab r r' := wl r r'`. For an unblocked node `ν` let `rec ν` be its pattern
(depth ≥ 2) or `(type (π ν), inr (r, wl (π ν) r))` (depth 1, below `r`).
`G := {rec ν | ν unblocked}`. Then:

* `G` is self-supporting: the support of `rec ν` is `E` := its `core`
  slots (uncounted by C5, distinct, never the anchor) and `C` := its
  `child` slots. A blocked child has the pattern of an unblocked ancestor,
  so its record is in `G`. `StarOK` holds because the neighbours are
  distinct model neighbours of `π ν` (slots are distinct witnesses, the
  parent's image is excluded), carry witness labels, and contain every
  witness of `π ν`.
* every root `r` satisfies the core condition with `C r` := its `child`
  slots: its core neighbours are distinct roots and its children have
  distinct images outside `R` (C5), so all its neighbours are distinct
  model neighbours, and its witnesses lie in `R ∪ children`.
* `NomOK`: nominal elements are roots; no other root has a nominal name
  (a nominal name holds at one element only).

**Lemma C7 (records equal patterns).** Two nodes of depth ≥ 2 with the
same pattern have the same record. This is why blocking by patterns
suffices even though nodes also have core entries: core entries are part
of the *support*, not of the record, so a blocked node is represented by
its blocker's support.

`realisation_complete` follows: C4 gives a final structure, C6 a
realisation over `K = R`, C3 the bound.

**Finite case (prototype, `cert.py`).** For a finite model the simpler
iteration "start with the nominal elements; while some element has, in the
witness unravelling relative to the core, a counted core entry, add it to
the core" terminates (the core is a subset of `D`) and yields a
certificate. It does not give the bound, but it exercises Lemmas C5–C7 on
every satisfiable random input (section 9).

### 3.5 Why not a bound from the model directly

The closure of the nominal elements under "counted neighbour" can be
infinite in a model (a two-way infinite chain through `o` with an
inverse functional role), and an element counted at a core element is
harmless when it is that element's tree child and occurs nowhere else.
Rudolph and Glimm (JAIR 39, 2010) show that models can force infinitely
many "new nominals" and remove them by an infinite approximation
argument that gives no explicit bound. The steered construction avoids
both problems: tree parts are copies, so the only shared elements are
the roots that NI introduces, and blocking bounds their depth.

## 4. Procedures

Over `Fin n` names and `Fin m` roles. `aliveN O ty` is the elimination of
`SRIQ/Procedure.lean` over `RecN (Fin k)` (a `Fintype`), with
`supBN G ρ` deciding "a core entry list and a children list of length at
most `bnd O` exist" (`support_bound` extends: removing core entries or
children that count for no lower clause keeps `StarOK`, and core entries
are at most one per core element). `good_iff_mem_aliveN` as
`good_iff_mem_alive`.

### 4.1 Hinted checkers

* `checkRealisationN O h`, `h = (k, ty, lab, kids)` with explicit core
  children lists: `0 < k`, `NomOK`, label symmetry and up-closure,
  `StarOK` of each core star, every child record in `aliveN O ty`.
  **Sound for every hint** (`checkRealisationN_sound`: `true →
  ConsistentN O`, from `realisation_sound`). Complete: if a realisation over
  `Fin k` exists, the hint made of it checks.
* `checkCertN O cert`: the same core data plus an explicit finite record
  table (`rid ↦ (t, parent, core entries, children as (L, rid))`), checked
  locally (no `aliveN`, linear in the certificate; `cert.py`
  `check_cert`). Sound because the table's records form a self-supporting
  set (`SelfSuppN` of the image of the table). Complete: `aliveN` with its
  chosen supports is such a table. This is the form km should export (its
  model: completion-graph roots as the core, its blockable nodes as
  records).

### 4.2 Exhaustive search

`searchN O : Bool := ∃ k ∈ [1, B O], ∃ ty labels, checks` with the core
enumerated as in `realise.py`: a partition of `Nom O` into blocks (one
core element per block), types containing exactly the block's nominal
names, `j` extra core elements with tree types in nondecreasing order,
labels from the minimal-label set (Lemma L), children lists up to
`bnd O` over `aliveN` candidates. `searchN_iff : searchN O = true ↔
ConsistentN O` from `consistentN_iff_realisation` and Lemma L. It defines
the answer and is far too slow for any real input (`B O` is doubly
exponential); `decide` on a `Fintype` as `SearchProp` in C1b.

`consistentN O hint := hint.any (checkRealisationN O) || searchN O`;
`consistentN_iff` for every hint, as `consistentKB_iff`.

### 4.3 What km needs to provide

A consistent answer is certified by a hint (`checkCertN`), an inconsistent
or entailed answer by km's soundness proof (`kmSupervisor_sound`) or by
the reference search where it finishes. So certification of km answers
does not need `B O`; only the completeness theorem of the reference does.

## 5. Reductions of the other rows to consistency

With nominals the TBox answer depends on the ABox and on the nominals, so
C1b's `entailsSubKB_iff` (disjoint union) does not carry over: every row
reduces to consistency with a fresh nominal, as the SRIQD KB rows do with
fresh individuals.

| Row | Reduction (`X` a fresh name) | Lemma |
|---|---|---|
| `a ⊑ b` | not entailed iff `O ++ [nom X, gci [X] [a], gci [X, b] []]` is consistent | `entailsSubN_iff` |
| `a` unsatisfiable | iff `O ++ [nom X, gci [X] [a]]` is inconsistent | `unsatN_iff` |
| `C(i)` | entailed iff `O ++ [gci [N_i, C] []]` is inconsistent | `entailsTypeN_iff` |
| `i = j` | entailed iff `O ++ [gci [N_i, N_j] []]` is inconsistent | `entailsSameN_iff` |
| `i ≠ j` | entailed iff `O ++ [gci [N_i] [N_j]]` is inconsistent (with `N_j ⊑ N_i` implied by `nom`) | `entailsDiffN_iff` |
| `R(i, j)` | entailed iff `O ++ [allRight N_i R nN_j, nN_j ⊓ N_j ⊑ ⊥, ⊤ ⊑ N_j ⊔ nN_j]` is inconsistent | `entailsRoleN_iff` |

Proofs: interpret `X` as `{x}` for the element `x` that witnesses the
non-entailment; conversely the element of `X`. One lemma per row over the
normal form, about 30 lines each (the `liftClause`/`complO` machinery of
`SRIQ/Realization.lean` gives the fresh names).

**One realisation, many rows.** In a certificate every record reachable
from a core child is realised in the model, so a certificate whose core
types and reachable record types contain a type `t` witnesses `a ⋢ b` for
all `a ∈ t`, `b ∉ t`. km can certify all non-subsumptions with a few
certificates instead of one per pair.

## 6. Data (SRIQD)

Data values have no inverse and are not shared between object elements
in any counted way: data stars depend on the centre type only
(`SRIQD/ABox.lean`). So the C4 reduction carries over unchanged: forbid
every type without a data star by one gci (`reduceWith`), encode data
assertions on individual `i` as clauses on its nominal name `N_i`
(`N_i ⊑ ∃u.L`, `N_i ⊑ ∀u.¬L`, as `SRIQD/ABoxProcedure.lean` `encO` does
with its fresh names, which become `nom` names here), and run the object
procedure. `consistentKBDN_iff` under `OracleSpec`, and under
`OracleSpecB Θ card B` with `dbnd ≤ B` as in `SRIQD/KBB.lean`. The proof
of `models_reduceWith_iff` is per element and does not look at nominals;
`exists_dmodel` builds values for all elements of one model, nominal or
not. Data nominals (`DataOneOf`) are data atoms with `card = 1` already.
Expected size: about 300 lines, mostly restating C4 lemmas for `SRIQN`.

## 7. Stage C2a: no inverse-counted needs

**Definition.** `IsC2a O`: for every lower clause `exRight a S b` or
`atLeast a k S b` with `a ∉ Nom O`, and every `atMost a' k' R b'`:
`¬ RoleLe O S R.flip`.

**Theorem C2a.** For `IsALCHOIQ O ∧ IsC2a O`: `ConsistentN O` iff a
realisation exists whose core elements each carry a nominal name (one per
block of a partition of `Nom O`; one arbitrary element if `Nom O = ∅`).
Moreover `|K| ≤ max(1, |Nom O|)`.

**Proof.** Soundness is section 3.2. Completeness needs no construction.
Take a model `I`, `K` := the nominal elements (or one element `x0` if
`Nom O = ∅`), core labels `wl`, and for a non-core element `x` reached from
a parent `y` (a core element for anchor records, else a non-core element
with `x ∈ wit y`) the support

* core entries: `(d, up (need x d))` for `d ∈ wit x ∩ K`, `d ≠ y`;
* children: `wit x \ (K ∪ {y})`, with witness labels.

`up (need x d)` is up-closed, lies inside the model label and covers the
needs of `x` met by `d`, so `StarOK` holds at `x` as in
`starOK_of_model`. The entry is counted at `d` only if some `atMost` at
`d` has a role `R` with `R.flip ∈ up (need x d)`, that is `S ⊑* R.flip`
for the role `S` of a lower clause whose subject holds at `x`. That
subject is not a nominal name (`x` is not a nominal element), so `IsC2a`
excludes it. The records of all such pairs `(x, y)` form a
self-supporting set (as `modelRecs_selfSupp`); core elements get their
non-core witnesses as children. No blocking, no promotion, no bound
beyond `|K| ≤ max(1, |Nom O|)`. About the size of C1b's
`realOfModel`/`consistentKB_iff_realisation`.

**Search.** Partitions of `Nom O` into blocks, block types, labels; no
extra core elements. On ABox-heavy inputs the hint (km's model) is the
only practical route, as in C1b.

**Census (section 10).** C2a holds on 208 of 276 analysed pool inputs
with nominals; with the 65 `SameIndividual`/`DifferentIndividuals`-only
inputs (C1b), C2a covers 273 of the 342 inputs that need any nominal
reasoning (80%).

## 8. Transitivity

`encTransQ` extends to `SRIQN` with `nom` clauses passed through on the
old names. Both model maps of `encTransQ_correct` leave concept
extensions of old names unchanged (the down map interprets the fresh
names, the up map closes transitive roles), so `nom` clauses are
preserved in both directions. `IsSHOIQ O`: `IsSHIQ` of the base clauses.
Negative role assertions become `allRight` clauses on `N_i`, so the C1b
side condition (simple roles in `nrole`) becomes the usual condition that
`allRight` over a non-simple role is handled by `encTrans`; nothing new.

## 12. Open points and uncertainty

* **U1 (completeness proof, section 3.4).** Pen proof only. The parts I
  am least sure of: (a) Lemma C4's claim that a deleted position is never
  re-created needs the slot bookkeeping exactly as stated (a root slot
  turns `core` when its witness becomes a root; a node slot turns `core`
  only by NI on its child); (b) C6's claim that the neighbours of a node
  have pairwise distinct images, all model neighbours of its image: the
  slots are distinct witnesses and exclude the parent's image, a `core d`
  slot has image `d`, and a self-loop (`x ∈ wit x`) gives a child whose
  image equals the node's own image, which is allowed (the node is not its
  own neighbour). A formal proof must state these cases.
* **U2 (levels).** Motik et al. define the level of a root as the length
  of a shortest root path, which can decrease, and count roots as trees of
  depth ℘ per named individual. With dynamic levels that count needs the
  introduction tree to have depth at most the level, which their text does
  not argue. I use the static introduction depth with re-parenting
  (NI lowers `idep y` when a shorter introduction appears), which keeps
  Lemma C2 and gives C3 directly. I believe this repairs the step, but it
  is my own argument.
* **U3 (the bound).** `B O` is doubly exponential and only defines the
  exhaustive search; no run will reach it. The prototype's exhaustive
  search goes to `|Nom| + 2` or `+ 3` core elements; its "no realisation"
  answers are cross-checked by HermiT and km, not by the bound.
* **U4 (C2a census).** The census test runs on km's DL clauses, not on the
  Lean translation; a different structural transformation can move an
  input in or out of C2a (for example a different encoding of
  `ObjectOneOf` with several individuals). The Lean translation must
  compute `IsC2a` itself and decline or route to the general stage.
* **U5 (singly exponential core).** Tobies (2000) and Pratt-Hartmann (2005)
  give NExpTime upper bounds through C2 and integer programming. A
  realisation-based singly exponential bound would need their counting
  arguments; not attempted.
* **U6 (prototype limits).** The exhaustive search times out on hand cases
  with ten or more names; those cases are decided by HermiT, km, the
  brute-force search and the certificate built from the finite model
  (section 9). The Python prototype is unverified code.
* **U7 (Lean statement of B).** The count of tree types and labels in ℘
  should use the minimal labels of Lemma L to keep the formula honest
  for the search; the proof of C1 works with any finite pattern set.
* **U8 (interaction with C3).** Self, disjoint roles and general chains
  are out of scope; the automaton encoding of chains (C3) adds fresh
  names and `allRight` clauses only, so it should compose with this
  stage, but Self restrictions on nominals need a separate look.
