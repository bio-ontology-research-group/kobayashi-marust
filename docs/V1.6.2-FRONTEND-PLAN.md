# KM v1.6.2 plan: the frontend in Lean

Status: plan, written 2026-11-12 during v1.6.1. Roadmap row (v1.5.6, carried
over): "Prove the frontend in Lean. A theorem must connect the clause set to
the source ontology under the OWL 2 direct semantics, covering IRI identity,
normalisation, and the regular role-inclusion encoding."

## 1. The gap

Every KM theorem up to v1.6.1 is stated on the frontend's normalised clause
stream (`List FCL`, `KMCommonRoutingSource.Entails`). The frontend
(`engine/src/frontend/`, 38,700 lines of Rust: parsing, prefix expansion,
builtin aliasing, rewrites, normalisation, role-chain and transitivity
encodings, data handling) is trusted. A wrong clause stream produces a wrong
answer that every certificate accepts.

## 2. The theorem

For an OFN document `text` inside the fragment `F` (section 3):

```lean
-- the parser is the definition of what the text means (IRI identity included)
OWL.parse text = some O
-- the Lean frontend
OWLFrontend.translate O = some T          -- T.clauses : List FCL, T.index : IRI → Option Nat
-- correctness
theorem translate_correct :
  ∀ A B ∈ O.classNames,
    OWL.EntailsSub O A B ↔ KMCommonRoutingSource.Entails T.clauses (T.index A) (T.index B)
```

with `OWL.EntailsSub` the OWL 2 direct semantics (interpretations with
nonempty domain, class and object-property extensions, the standard
constructors) for the fragment, and the analogous statement for
unsatisfiable classes and ontology inconsistency.

Two consumers:

1. **Reference path, end to end.** `km-el-reference --owl <file>` parses and
   translates in Lean, then runs the v1.6.0/v1.6.1 reference procedures on
   `T`. Capstone `kmSupervisorOWL_complete_on_EL_or_ALC`: for every OFN text
   in the OWL EL or ALC part of `F`, the supervisor publishes, and the answer
   is exact under the OWL direct semantics of the text. This needs a second
   theorem that ties the fragments: `translate` of an OWL-EL ontology is
   accepted by `recognizeEL` (and the ALC analogue), proved rather than
   measured as in v1.6.0's P2.
2. **Rust routes, checked.** `km-frontend-check <text> <rust clause stream>
   <definer map>` accepts when the Rust stream equals Lean's `T` up to the
   renaming of fresh symbols given by the definer map (both sides key fresh
   names by the structurally equal subconcept they abbreviate, as the Rust
   normaliser documents). Theorem: acceptance implies the `translate_correct`
   statement for the Rust stream, so `kmSupervisor_sound` lifts to OWL for
   every published Rust answer whose stream was checked. The check runs only
   when enabled (`KM_FRONTEND_CHECK`), like the v1.5.5 checkers, so S1 is
   unaffected; its cost is measured.

## 3. The fragment

Survey 2026-11-12 (`/mnt/data1/leechuck/km-work/v161/survey/REPORT.md`,
1,781 checkers-on inputs, all but one in functional syntax; no OWL/XML):

| fragment | all inputs | v1.6.0-recognised (592) |
|---|---:|---:|
| EL | 237 | 223 |
| EL + role hierarchy + transitivity + chains | 388 | 364 |
| ALC | 264 | 223 |
| ALC + role hierarchy + inverses | 311 | 236 |
| ALC + role hierarchy + inverses + transitivity + chains (`F`) | 499 | 389 |
| `F` + class and object-property assertions | (stage 2) | |

`F` is the choice: SubClassOf, EquivalentClasses, DisjointClasses,
ObjectPropertyDomain/Range, SubObjectPropertyOf with atomic roles and
chains, EquivalentObjectProperties, InverseObjectProperties,
ObjectInverseOf, Transitive and SymmetricObjectProperty; class expressions
over names, owl:Thing, owl:Nothing, IntersectionOf, UnionOf, ComplementOf,
SomeValuesFrom, AllValuesFrom; Declarations, annotations and prefixes. On
an `F` input the Rust passes that can fire are conformance, parse, the
bottom prepass (off the ELC route), normalisation, chain and transitivity
augmentation, domain/range clauses, inverse-bridge pruning and the ELC
demotion check; no builtin-alias rewrite fires anywhere in the suite, and
prefixed and full spellings of one IRI never co-occur, but the Lean parser
expands prefixes and compares expanded IRIs anyway. ABox assertions (147
recognised inputs are blocked by ClassAssertion alone) are stage 2, after
`F` is done; cardinalities, nominals, data, rules and the top role are out.

## 5. The proof obligation that needs design first

FCL is first-order, so a role inclusion `R∘S ⊑ T` and transitivity are
ordinary clauses (`R(x,y) ∧ S(y,z) → T(x,z)`). The Lean translation keeps
them: `T = Base ∪ Ch`, where `Base` is the structural transformation of the
class axioms and simple role axioms and `Ch` the chain and transitivity
clauses. `translate_correct` for `Base ∪ Ch` is then a model-level argument
(definers expand and restrict) with no role-encoding content.

The Rust stream differs: `preprocess::augment_with_chains_retaining_role_axioms`
drops `Ch` on the default routes (`keep_chains` is false unless rules, the
role automaton or nominal ABoxes are on) and adds `Derived`: propagation
predicates per transitive role and consumed concept set
(`__trans__T__{C}`: `T(x,y) ∧ ⋀C(y) → P(x)`, `T(x,y) ∧ P(y) → P(x)`,
`Γ(x) ∧ P(x) → Δ(x)`), the chain analogues, the regular-automaton consumers
and the domain consumers. So the Rust stream is `Base ∪ Derived` and the
statement to prove is class-entailment equivalence, not model equivalence:

```
Entails (Base ∪ Derived) A B ↔ Entails (Base ∪ Ch) A B      (A, B named)
```

- (⇐) `Base ∪ Ch ∪ Derived` is a conservative extension of `Base ∪ Ch`:
  interpret each `P = __trans__T__{C}` as `∃T.⋀C` (its clauses hold in
  every model of `Ch`). So `Base ∪ Derived` is entailed by `Base ∪ Ch` up to
  the fresh symbols, and every subsumption of `Base ∪ Derived` holds in
  `Base ∪ Ch`.
- (⇒) From a model `I` of `Base ∪ Derived`, build `I*` with the same domain
  and concept extensions, and role extensions closed under `Ch` (least
  fixpoint). `I*` satisfies `Ch` by construction and agrees with `I` on
  every concept. It satisfies a clause of `Base` whose body has no role atom
  of a role `Ch` enlarges, and every clause with role atoms only in its
  head (more edges only help). The remaining clauses are the *consumers* of
  non-simple roles (`T(x,y) ∧ C(y) → D(x)` and the domain shape
  `T(x,y) → D(x)`); for each new edge of `I*`, the propagation clauses of
  `Derived` give the required head in `I`, by induction on the derivation of
  the edge in the closure. So the theorem holds exactly when every consumer
  of a non-simple role is covered by its `Derived` family: a decidable,
  syntactic *coverage condition* on `Base ∪ Derived`.

The Lean module states the coverage condition as a checkable predicate,
proves the equivalence under it, and the frontend checker evaluates it on
the Rust stream (so a Rust consumer shape the encoding misses is a rejected
check, not a wrong theorem). The closure induction carries the regular
automaton: an edge of `I*` is a word of base edges accepted by the role's
automaton, and the propagation predicates are the automaton's states.

## 7. Revision 2026-11-12: phase A first, on the reference procedures' own normal forms

Reading the v1.6.0 recognisers changes the order of work. `recognizeEL`
accepts the clause shapes of NF1 to NF7 (role inclusions `nf6`, binary
chains `nf7` and reflexive roles included); `recognizeALC` inverts
`CBALCEncoding.encode` of the `Ctx` normal clauses (`gci`, `exRight`,
`exLeft`, `allRight`). Both reference procedures decide exactly on those
normal forms (`referenceBit_exact`, `alcBit_exact`). So the Lean frontend
does not need to reproduce the Rust normaliser's clause stream to get an
OWL-level theorem: it normalises OWL directly into the normal forms the
reference procedures decide, and the existing encoders (`elcOntology`,
`CBALCEncoding.encode`) with their theorems give the clause-level reading.

**Phase A (v1.6.2).**

```lean
namespace ContextCalculus.OWLFrontend

-- EL route: OWL EL with role hierarchy, chains (any length; fresh roles
-- split chains of length > 2), transitivity, domains; no inverses,
-- no symmetry, no ranges, no union, no complement, no universal.
structure ELTranslation where
  named : List OWL.IRI          -- the requested IRIs, duplicates removed; IRI named[i] has index i
  top : Nat
  bottom : Nat
  ontology : ELCompletion.Ontology Nat Nat

def translateEL (O : OWL.Ontology) (requested : List OWL.IRI) : Option ELTranslation

theorem translateEL_named (h : translateEL O requested = some T) :
    T.named = requested.eraseDups
theorem translateEL_isSome (hEL : OWL.Ontology.isEL O = true) :
    (translateEL O requested).isSome = true
theorem translateEL_correct (h : translateEL O requested = some T)
    (i j : Nat) (hi : i < T.named.length) (hj : j < T.named.length) :
    OWL.EntailsSub O T.named[i] T.named[j] ↔
      ELCompletion.EntailsSub (top := T.top) (bottom := T.bottom) T.ontology i j

-- ALC route: OWL ALC (all class constructors of F, domains, ranges),
-- no role axioms, no inverses.
structure ALCTranslation where
  named : List OWL.IRI
  conceptCount : Nat
  roleCount : Nat
  hnamed : named.length ≤ conceptCount
  ontology : Ctx.Ontology (Fin conceptCount) (Fin roleCount)

def translateALC (O : OWL.Ontology) (requested : List OWL.IRI) : Option ALCTranslation
-- the same three theorems, `translateALC_correct` against
-- `CBALCEncoding.EntailsSub T.ontology ⟨i, _⟩ ⟨j, _⟩`.

-- The OWL reference procedure and its capstone.
def owlReference (O : OWL.Ontology) (requested : List OWL.IRI) : Option OWLTaxonomy
theorem owlReference_sound :
    owlReference O requested = some t →
      t.named = requested.eraseDups ∧
      ∀ A ∈ t.named, ∀ B ∈ t.named, (t.bit A B = true ↔ OWL.EntailsSub O A B)
theorem owlReference_complete (h : O.isEL = true ∨ O.isALC = true) :
    (owlReference O requested).isSome = true      -- up to the ALC signature budget, as in v1.6.0
```

Unsatisfiability and inconsistency need no extra statement: requested
IRIs may be fresh, a fresh `A` is unsatisfiable-equivalent to
`EntailsSub O A B` for a fresh `B` exactly when `O` is inconsistent, and
`A` is unsatisfiable exactly when it is subsumed by a fresh IRI that
appears nowhere else (the Rust probes use the same reading).

Normalisation (both routes): structural transformation with definers keyed
by the structurally equal subexpression *and its polarity* (a negative
occurrence `E` gets `E ⊑ X_E⁻`, a positive one `X_E⁺ ⊑ E`). Correctness
is the standard two-way argument: (⇐) a model of `O` extends to a model of
the normal form by `X_E± := ext E`; (⇒) a model `J` of the normal form
restricts to a model of `O`, using the lemma, by induction on `E`, that
`ext E x → J(X_E⁻) x` for negative and `J(X_E⁺) x → ext E x` for positive
occurrences. Named IRIs take the indices `0 .. named.length - 1`; definers,
fresh chain roles, top and bottom take indices above.

`km-el-reference --owl <file>` runs parse, translate and the reference
procedures. For the Rust routes, phase A certifies answers, not streams:
with `KM_FRONTEND_CHECK=1` the Rust answer is compared with
`owlReference` on the same text, and agreement makes the Rust answer
exact under the OWL direct semantics by `owlReference_sound`.

**Phase B (stream checking, sections 2 and 5).** The checker of the Rust
clause stream up to definer renaming, with the coverage condition and the
role-encoding equivalence, remains the plan for Rust answers on inputs
outside the reference procedures' fragments (inverses, symmetry, ranges
with EL). It follows phase A in v1.6.2 if time allows, or moves to
v1.6.3 with the hypertableau completeness work that needs the same role
reasoning.
