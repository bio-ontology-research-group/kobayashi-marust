# P1: why KM's median peak memory went from 24-26 MiB to 68-72 MiB

Date: 2026-11-14. Author: P1 diagnosis agent. No builds, no edits to any worktree, one km run at a time, KM_THREADS=1 unless stated.
Scripts and raw data: `/mnt/data1/leechuck/km-work/v170/scratch/p1/` (probe.py, probe2.py, emul.py, timeline.py, out/*.json, out/emul*.jsonl).
Marked "(inferred)" = not directly measured.

## Short answer

1. The jump is mostly real, and one mechanism explains most of it: every release build since v1.5.1 uses the mimalloc allocator (`--features mimalloc` in `tools/release/v155/pgo_build.py:151`). On a machine where Linux transparent huge pages (THP) are set to `always`, mimalloc's memory gets backed by 2 MiB huge pages and each KM process shows 20 to 30 MiB of extra resident memory. v1.4.5 and the current int build (v1.6.4-rc1 from `target-int`) use the glibc allocator and do not show it. With THP switched off for the process, v1.6.2 (mimalloc) drops from a median of 44.5 MiB to 25.3 MiB on my inputs, the same as the glibc build (24.8).
2. The monitor changed too, but it explains only a small part (about 5 MiB on my inputs for the same binary; larger on busy Ibex nodes, inferred). The peak rule is the same in both eras: maximum of (sum of RSS over the process tree, sampled every 20 ms) and (`/usr/bin/time` max RSS of the biggest single process). What changed is how often and how completely short runs were sampled.
3. Smaller real growth: routes that ran inside one process in v1.4.5 now start a second worker process (supervisor plus `km tableau`). Each process maps the 15 to 19 MB binary and the harness counts those shared pages once per process. Size of the biggest single process grew only 11.8 to 13.3 MiB (+1.5 MiB) over the same period.
4. After removing the THP effect KM is at parity with Konclude on small inputs, not below it. Getting clearly under Konclude needs the two further changes in section 5.

## 1. What each era's monitor measured

Both eras run the reasoner as `/usr/bin/time -f %M -o max-rss-kib.txt <km classify ...>` in its own session (`setsid`), pinned to one CPU, and report `peak_bytes = max(sampled_peak, time %M)`.

v1.4.5 era ("unscoped"), `paper/benchmark/comparative/measure_classification.py`:
- line 126: `measured_command = ['/usr/bin/time', '-f', '%M', '-o', ...] + command`
- lines 130-131: `watchdog.monitor(proc, timeout=..., memcap_bytes=..., sample_interval=.02, on_trip=checkpoint)`
- lines 132-135: `peak = result.peak_bytes` then `peak = max(peak, int(line) * 1024)` from the time file.
- `oracle/ore/tree_watchdog.py` line 90 `snapshot_all()` reads `/proc/<every pid on the node>/stat` field 24 (RSS pages) in Python; line 107 `tree_and_group_rss()` sums RSS over descendants (by PPID) plus the process group; line 367 `time.sleep(sample_interval)`.
- So: sum of RSS over the tree (the `time` wrapper, the supervisor and each worker), one full /proc scan per 20 ms plus the scan cost, and the biggest-single-process high-water mark from `time`.

v1.5.3+ era ("scoped watchdog"), the protocol copy pinned by hash in every Ibex record (`measure_classification.py` sha 1bb5105..., found at `.work/agent-scratch/data-values/v153tools/`):
- line 128 same `/usr/bin/time -f %M` wrapper; lines 132-133 `start_exit_observer(proc, poll_interval_s=0.001)` and `ScopedSnapshot(proc.pid)`; lines 143-146 the same `peak = max(sampled, time %M)` rule.
- `scoped_snapshot.py` lines 37-58: lists pids from the worker's cgroup (`cgroup.procs`, walked recursively) instead of the whole /proc, then the same `tree_and_group_rss()` filter. `tree_watchdog.py` (monitor-scope-fix copy) line 369-372: `wake_event.wait(sample_interval)` so the loop wakes on exit through a `pidfd`.
- Changelog `CHANGELOG.md` ("[1.5.3]", about line 596) says the old poller took CPU on the pinned core at 50 Hz and added up to 20 ms exit latency. That is a timing change and a sampling-density change. It is not a different definition of memory.

Net: the quantity is "RSS summed over the tree, not PSS, not cgroup peak" in both eras. A faster, scoped sampler sees the supervisor and the worker alive at the same time more often, so the sum rises (about +5 MiB median for the same v1.4.5 binary in my replay: old monitor 12.4 MiB, new monitor 17.7 MiB). On a loaded Ibex node the old monitor was slower, so its undercount was probably larger (inferred). RSS counts the shared binary and libc pages once per process, so two processes cost about 2 x 10 MiB of file-backed pages before any heap.

## 2. Per-binary table, one method

Method: the real `tree_watchdog.py` / `scoped_snapshot.py` / `timing_support.py` modules, unmodified, driven by `emul.py` (setsid, one CPU pinned, `/usr/bin/time %M`, 20 ms sampling, same env as the Ibex runner including the five release flags and `KM_JSON_GRAPH_EDGES=1`). 15 inputs under 1 s (14 corpus inputs of 29 KB to 217 KB plus a 3-axiom ontology), 3 repetitions, median of repetitions, then median over inputs. THP on this machine: `enabled=[always]`, `defrag=[madvise]` (same as I expect on Ibex, inferred).

Binaries: v1.4.5 = `v145-candidates/km-v145-global-dependency` (the 2026-10-04 comparative candidate, no mimalloc, 15.5 MB); v1.5.1 to v1.6.2 = the release assets (all contain mimalloc, PGO from v1.5.3; the v1.5.3 asset is byte-identical to the Ibex binary, sha 2470b04...); v1.6.4-rc1 = `target-int/release/km` (glibc, no PGO, 18.5 MB; the Ibex rc binary is PGO plus mimalloc, so this one is NOT the Ibex build).

Median over the 15 inputs, MiB (lower is better):

| Binary | allocator | old monitor, THP on | new monitor, THP on | new monitor, THP off | biggest single process, THP off |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| v1.4.5 | glibc | 12.4 | 17.7 | 17.7 | 11.8 |
| v1.5.1 | mimalloc | 16.7 | 25.7 | 24.9 | 13.8 |
| v1.5.3 | mimalloc + PGO | 29.1 | 34.2 | 21.4 | 13.7 |
| v1.5.4 | mimalloc + PGO | 27.7 | 32.4 | 24.4 | 12.9 |
| v1.5.5 | mimalloc + PGO | 32.1 | 44.0 | 22.8 | 13.3 |
| v1.6.2 | mimalloc + PGO | 31.4 | 44.5 | 25.3 | 13.2 |
| v1.6.4-rc1 (int) | glibc | 14.6 | 24.5 | 24.8 | 13.3 |

Reading it:
- Column 4 (new monitor, THP on) is what Ibex's protocol measures: 17.7 at v1.4.5 up to 44.5 at v1.6.2. This reproduces the direction of the Ibex record (26 to 68).
- Column 5 (THP off): all binaries sit between 21 and 25 MiB from v1.5.1 on. The release-to-release growth disappears. So the growth from v1.5.x to v1.6.2 in column 4 is THP plus mimalloc, not code growth.
- v1.4.5 to v1.5.1 (17.7 to 24.9 with THP off): a real +7 MiB. Cause: routing. In v1.4.5 inputs 10320 and 10019 ran inside the supervisor (1 process, 11.5 and 16.7 MiB). From v1.5.1 on, 10320 starts `km tableau` as a second process (2 processes, 41 MiB summed). Mean process count over my ten matrix inputs stays at 1.7 to 1.9 for all versions; the change is which inputs get a second process.
- Column 6 (biggest process) is flat: 11.8 to 13.3 MiB. No code or data bloat in the typical process (+1.5 MiB over six releases).
- Old monitor vs new monitor for the same v1.4.5 binary: 12.4 vs 17.7. This is the measurement change in isolation (+5.3 MiB median locally).

Check against Ibex on the same held-out inputs (rc run, `v164/measure/data/heldout-rc-pair55574426`, 11 inputs, MiB):

| | Ibex KM rc (PGO+mimalloc) | Ibex KM v1.5.3 | Ibex Konclude | local v1.6.2, THP on | local v1.6.2, THP off | local rc1 glibc |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| median of 11 | 61.2 | 63.1 | 28.8 | 45.3 | 25.3 | 24.9 |

Correlation of Ibex rc with local v1.6.2 (THP on) across the 11 inputs: 0.94; with local glibc rc1: 0.83. Per input examples (Ibex rc, local v1.6.2 THP on, THP off): 7962: 61.2, 60.4, 27.2; 10807: 97.2, 98.3, 51.7; 1222: 57.4, 41.6, 16.6; 15514: 69.5, 57.7, 30.9. Ibex values sit about 1.35 times above my local THP-on values (inferred: more cores, other kernel or slower sampling), but the shape is the THP-on shape, not the glibc shape.

Signature of the mechanism: the identical command `v1.6.2 classify ore_ont_7962` gives max-process RSS of 13.4, 21.6 or 41.6 MiB in repeated runs, and `AnonHugePages` of up to 30 MiB per process. With `prctl(PR_SET_THP_DISABLE)` set before exec: 12.9 to 13.4 MiB, 0 MiB huge pages, in all 5 repetitions. The glibc build never shows huge pages (0 MiB in 40 runs). mimalloc environment switches (`MIMALLOC_ARENA_EAGER_COMMIT=0`, `MIMALLOC_PURGE_DELAY=0`, `MIMALLOC_ALLOW_LARGE_OS_PAGES=0`) did not remove it. The same bimodal behaviour is a plausible cause of the "median drifts 1.14 to 1.31 across runs" noted in the plan (inferred).

Why mimalloc plus THP inflates (inferred from smaps): mimalloc reserves a 1 GiB virtual arena (`anon mmap` mapped 1.05 to 1.12 GiB in smaps) and hands out 64 KiB pages per size class from 32 MiB segments. Small allocations in many size classes touch many 2 MiB regions; with THP `always` the kernel backs each touched region with a full 2 MiB page. glibc's brk heap is contiguous, so only the used part is touched.

## 3. Today's footprint on tiny inputs (v1.6.4-rc1 int build, glibc, KM_THREADS=1)

3-axiom ontology (`in/tiny3.ofn`): 1 process, 1 thread, 7.9 MiB RSS (probe2 smaps):
- binary code (r-xp, 15 MB mapped): 4.74 MiB resident (about 60%). It is file-backed, shared, and faulted in as the program runs (kernel fault-around maps 64 KiB neighbours).
- binary read-only data and relocations: 0.83 MiB.
- libc/libm/libgcc: 1.64 + 0.55 MiB (PSS of these is about 0.04 MiB, they are shared).
- heap (brk): 0.03 MiB; other anonymous: 0.05 MiB; stack 0.06 MiB.
- Nothing is allocated up front: `km routes` (no ontology) is 2.7 MiB (v1.4.5: 2.7; v1.6.2 mimalloc: 3.6 to 5.6). The 5 MiB between "no ontology" and "3 axioms" is code pages touched by parsing, normalising and classifying.
- Thread stacks: none of consequence. Small inputs have 1 to 5 threads. Inputs that run the HT race (11834, 7525) show 58 to 61 threads and VmPeak 4.1 GiB in the supervisor (`race.rs:428` uses `available_parallelism`; `mod.rs:1217` asks for 256 MiB stacks for the in-process CB thread), but RSS stays at 4 MiB anonymous. Stack reservation is virtual here and not a memory cost. KM_THREADS (unset, 1, 4) changed nothing on three inputs (10320: 18.5/18.4/18.6 MiB max-process; 11834 and 7525 within 0.8 MiB).
- Release flags (`routing.rs:3441-3447`, `RELEASE_DEFAULTS`: KM_HT_DDB, KM_HT_NATIVE_FULL, KM_CACHE_CONFORMANCE, KM_BRIDGE_SUBJECT_WORKERS_OVERRIDE, KM_FAST_IRI_GRAMMAR, plus the harness's KM_JSON_GRAPH_EDGES): on 7962, 10320, 16847 with the glibc build, base, five flags, graph edges, `KM_NO_RELEASE_DEFAULTS=1`, 1-CPU pinning and OMP/RAYON env gave identical numbers within 0.5 MiB (3 reps each). The flags are not a small-input cost. KM_CACHE_CONFORMANCE could matter on large inputs; not tested here.
- Allocator: see section 2. No mimalloc in the current int build; all v1.5.1 to v1.6.2 assets and, by the build script, every Ibex release binary contain it (`strings` finds 7 mimalloc strings; v1.4.5 and rc1-int have 0).

A typical two-process run (ore_ont_10320, 153 KB, SHOIQ, route `tableau`), timeline sampled every 2 ms:
- The supervisor reads and normalises the ontology, reaching 18 MiB RSS (8 MiB anonymous) in about 100 ms, then starts `km tableau`.
- The worker then reloads the clause file and grows from 0 to 18 MiB (8 to 9 MiB anonymous) in the next 85 ms, while the supervisor stays at 18 to 19 MiB. For ore_ont_10807 the supervisor holds 22 MiB (12 anonymous) while the worker climbs to 20 MiB.
- So the summed peak is about 2 x (10 MiB binary pages + working set): 38 MiB for 10320, 45 MiB for 10807. Konclude on 10320-like inputs is about 25 to 30 MiB.
- The supervisor's anonymous data is not freed while the worker runs. The existing hook `release_transient_heap()` (`engine_run.rs:355`, `mem.rs`) is switched off unless `KM_HEAP_TRIM=1`, and the router sets that only for one small nominals case (`orchestrate/mod.rs:3051`). Setting `KM_HEAP_TRIM=1` by hand changed nothing on five inputs (10320: 37.8 vs 38.4 summed MiB), and the hook does nothing at all under mimalloc (`mem.rs`: "no-op outside glibc"). So the retained 8 to 12 MiB is probably live data in the supervisor, not freed-but-unreturned heap (inferred; needs a heap profile to name it).

## 4. Responsible commits and mechanisms

- `d95f0b84` "release: prepare v1.5.0 verified performance build" adds the optional `mimalloc` feature (`engine/Cargo.toml:44,53-54`, `engine/src/bin/km.rs:23-25`). `docs/RELEASE-1.5.2.md:16`, `RELEASE-1.5.3.md:48,240`, `RELEASE-1.5.4.md:156`, `RELEASE-1.5.5-PROCEDURE.md:53` and `tools/release/v155/pgo_build.py:151` build every release with `--features mimalloc`. Plain `cargo build --release` (what `target-int` uses) does not. This is the main cause of the THP-dependent growth (real, in the shipped binary).
- v1.5.3 monitor change (`ScopedSnapshot` plus `pidfd` wake, `CHANGELOG.md` ~596; modules from commit c4b7e61b and the monitor-scope-fix work of 2026-10-20): measurement change, about +5 MiB median locally.
- v1.4.5 to v1.5.1 routing: SHOIQ/SHIQ inputs that ran in the supervisor now run a `km tableau` worker (two processes, binary pages counted twice, working set duplicated). Cause is the routing code and `orchestrate/engine_run.rs`, not one commit; I did not bisect.
- PGO (v1.5.3): not a cause. With THP off, v1.5.1 (no PGO) 24.9 and v1.5.3 (PGO) 21.4.
- Process count, threads, route: unchanged on average (about 1.8 processes per input).
- Binary size: 15.5 MB (v1.4.5), 17.6 (v1.5.1), 17.8, 18.5, 19.6, 19.7 (v1.6.2): +4 MB of code, but resident code per process grew only slightly.

## 5. Proposals to get under Konclude (Ibex median 45 to 48 MiB; 28.8 on the 11 small held-out inputs)

Ranked by expected saving per risk. Savings are local estimates, scaled to Ibex by my 1.35 factor where noted (inferred). Nothing implemented.

1. Switch THP off for KM processes by default: one `prctl(PR_SET_THP_DISABLE, 1, 0, 0, 0)` at the start of `main` in `km.rs` (inherited across fork and exec, so workers get it), and re-enable (`prctl(..., 0)`) only inside the heavy CB/EL workers when the clause count is large. Alternative: build releases without `--features mimalloc` (remove it in `pgo_build.py:151`). Expected saving: local median 44.5 to 25.3 MiB (-19 MiB, -43%); on Ibex about -20 to -27 MiB, bringing the 70.8 median to roughly 45 to 50 MiB (inferred), about equal to Konclude's 48.4. Risk: low for correctness (allocator hint only, no Lean impact). Performance risk on large inputs where THP or mimalloc helped; gate with the existing perf set (the mean-tail inputs). First step, no code: an Ibex job that prints `/sys/kernel/mm/transparent_hugepage/enabled` and `AnonHugePages` from `/proc/self/smaps_rollup` of a KM run, to confirm Ibex has THP `always`; then rerun 20 held-out inputs with and without `PR_SET_THP_DISABLE`.
2. Stop paying twice for the working set when a worker runs: free or never build the supervisor's copy of the clause data once it is written to the clause file, keeping only the IRI map needed for output. Expected saving: 8 to 12 MiB anonymous on 100 to 200 KB inputs, more on bigger ones (the median held-out input is 620 KB, so likely 15+ MiB, inferred), i.e. about -25% of the summed peak on two-process runs. Risk: medium; the supervisor may need the data for retries and races (`race.rs`, adaptive retry), so it must reload from the file on the rare fallback path. Needs a heap profile of the supervisor (dhat feature exists) to name what it keeps.
3. Run the small tableau/HT jobs inside the supervisor (like `inproc-cb` and the in-process EL path already do), or `exec` the worker in place of the supervisor when only one route will run. Expected saving: one whole process, about 9 to 10 MiB of shared binary and libc pages plus the duplicate working set (local: 10320 38 MiB to about 19 MiB, v1.4.5 did 12 MiB). Risk: highest of the three. Loses SIGKILL isolation, the memory cap poll and race cancellation for those routes; restrict to inputs under a size threshold and to single-engine routes, keep the process for races and big inputs.
4. Shrink the resident code: `madvise(MADV_RANDOM)` on the binary's text to cut fault-around (maps 16 pages per fault) or order functions hot-first; target 4.7 MiB resident code of the 7.9 MiB floor. Expected saving 1 to 3 MiB per process (inferred, untested). Risk: low, some extra page faults (few ms). Also worth trying `opt-level="s"` for cold modules only; larger risk for speed.
5. Measurement fairness (not a fix): record PSS and the cgroup `memory.peak` next to the RSS sum, and report Konclude the same way. PSS removes the double count of shared binary pages (my runs: sum RSS 38 MiB vs PSS 29 MiB for 10320). This lowers KM more than Konclude (single process) so the comparison must be disclosed as a different metric and not replace the RSS series.

Order to try: 1 (a day, decides most), then 2, then 3 only if the Ibex median is still above Konclude's. Even after 1 and 2 the small-input floor of two processes is about 20 MiB summed against Konclude's 24 to 29 MiB, so beating Konclude on the median needs proposal 3 or 2 on the larger half of the corpus.

## 6. Limits

- Local box (56 cores, shared, load about 20), not an Ibex node: absolute numbers are lower than Ibex by about 1.35 times; the ordering and the THP signature match (corr 0.94 with Ibex rc on 11 inputs).
- The Ibex rc binary (sha 4ee6147d...) is PGO plus mimalloc and I do not have it. v1.6.2 (same recipe) stands in. The Ibex kernel's THP setting is inferred.
- No v1.4.5 per-input memory record is available locally (Ibex only), so the 24 to 26 MiB figure was reproduced only by direction: v1.4.5 binary, old monitor, 12.4 MiB; new monitor, 17.7 MiB (on smaller inputs than the Ibex median).
- Inputs are 14 corpus files of 29 to 217 KB plus tiny3; the held-out median is 620 KB. Memory grows with input size (held-out medians by size: <50 KB 26.5 MiB, 50-200 KB 47.5, 200 KB-1 MB 62.6, 1-5 MB 84.8, >5 MB 275 for KM rc; Konclude 25.4, 27.2, 35.9, 80.3, 479.8).
- v1.4.5 on ore_ont_12566 ran for more than 20 s in my try (engine route) and was killed; that input is not in the tables for v1.4.5.
- nproc in the raw probe JSON includes the `/usr/bin/time` wrapper in some runs; section 4 process counts exclude it.
